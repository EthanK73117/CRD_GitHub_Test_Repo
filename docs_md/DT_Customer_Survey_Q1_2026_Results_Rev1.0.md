## Slide 4

**Background**

- CCG made a request to CMEE to obtain Desktop Customer feedback regarding HS mounting/design, physical constraints, TIM (thermal interface material), and ILM (independent loading mechanism) Requirements.
- Survey purpose:
- As we (CCG) are preparing to architect future platforms, our mechanical architecture team would like to understand how Intel customers are using our enabling solutions. The purpose of this survey is to grow better understanding on this topic so we can consider customer feedback during the design process
- 4 of the largest Desktop customers (OEMs) were surveyed: Asus, Dell, HP, and Lenovo.
- All OEMs, except HP, have provided responses
- Dell provided survey responses from both their Thermal Engineering and Services Engineering teams

## Slide 12

**Summary**

- The summarized results below include Asus, Dell (Thermal Eng.), Dell (Services Eng.), and Lenovo
- Heatsink Mounting & Design:
- 3/4 OEMs find value in removing PCB heatsink holes and directly attaching heatsink to ILM
- 3/4 OEMs find value in extending backplate for connecting both socket LM and heatsink anchors
- OEMs HS min/max load range: ~20lbf to ~53lbf
- 2/4 OEMs have experience with HS w/ pedestal in the cold plate. Some challenges from pedestal design are HS static loading, strain gage testing, CPU contact for hotspot, and Z-height stack up tolerance
- Common HS mechanical validation processes: Drop, Durability, Interference, Shock, Strain, Structural Analysis, Thermal, Tolerance, Vibration
- Height and Physical Constraints:
- 1/4 OEMs can accommodate a backplate thicker than 2.2mm. The available clearance from this customer is 2.54mm
- 3/4 OEMs PCB thickness for LGA product systems is ~1.55-1.6mm (1 of the 3 also mentioned 1.2mm)
- 1/4 OEMs provided potential issues between LM and other board components (e.g. HS hits VR component, like a cap or choke)
- ILM Requirements:
- 3/4 OEMs say that a lever actuated ILM (tool-less) is necessary for their high-performance applications & designs (1/4 says it depends on the system)
- 3/4 OEMs say that an ILM solution involving a tool is not acceptable
- 1/4 OEMs say they modify/customize the Intel certified ILM to address their system needs, but did not provide any further details
- Open Feedback:
- Asus requested strain gage and PCB deformation spec
- Dell (Thermal Eng.) OEMs requested the IHS deformation post ILM closure to be addressed
- Dell (Services Eng.) stated that the design is adequate for servicing and has no issues
- Lenovo requested:
- Better tolerance control, as they found the tolerance illustrations incomplete (leading to interference and breakage issues)
- Qualification of Socket Vendor and ILM vendor mixing (e.g. Socket Vendor A to work with ILM Vendor B for mass production)
